Planetary Analogue Discovery System

A 100% data-driven Machine Learning and Remote Sensing framework developed by Team Perseus combining two coupled engines—SYBIL (curated multi-domain calibration) and IRIS (cloud-native global planetary analog discovery)—to identify high-fidelity terrestrial testbeds for permanent Lunar and Martian surface bases.

About the Engineering Problem

Selecting future landing sites and permanent surface bases on the Moon and Mars requires matching complex planetary constraints—topography, geology, thermophysics, radiation, and resource availability—with terrestrial testbeds. However, no single site on Earth is a universal planetary analogue:

Volcanic & Geotechnical Fidelity

An environment ideal for rover traction on loose basaltic lapilli (e.g., Mount Etna) replicates slope stability and regolith abrasion, yet cannot simulate cryogenic temperatures or ultra-high vacuum.

Hyperarid & Radiative Fidelity

A hyperarid desert (e.g., Atacama Desert) replicates UV stress and low biomass for astrobiology, yet lacks permafrost or subsurface basaltic lava tubes.

Perseus bridges this gap by filtering and matching environments based on concrete operational objectives: Rover Mobility & Abrasion, Subsurface Lava-Tube Habitation, ISRU Water-Ice Extraction, High-Altitude Radiative Stress, and Astrobiology.

IRIS • Global Planetary Discovery Engine

IRIS: Global Planetary Analog Discovery

IRIS (IRIS_Flow_B/) extends SYBIL’s selection engine from a closed catalog of documented literature sites to an automated global planetary discovery system. Operating on Google Earth Engine (GEE) over a 4-year climatological window (2021–2024), the four-module Python pipeline (perseus_common.py, gee_global_extract.py, run_flow_B_discovery.py, and generate_local_viewer.py) scans Earth’s emerged landmass, derives 28 physical and geotechnical variables from NASA, ESA, and ECMWF missions (NASADEM, Copernicus GLO-30/CryoSat-2, Landsat 8 & 9, MODIS Aqua, ERA5-Land, and OpenLandMap), and projects abiotic candidate cells into SYBIL’s calibrated latent space to uncover novel Lunar and Martian analogues.

01 • Cascade Filtering

4-Barrier Global Screening

Starts from an equal-area global grid (0.25° step, ~656k cells), excludes known SYBIL sites within a 50 km Haversine radius, masks vegetated and urban areas at 1 km (MODIS NDVI < 0.20), enforces strict 30 m abiotic thresholds (Landsat NDVI < 0.15), and flags Lander (slope ≤ 5°) and Rover (traverse slope ≤ 12°) suitability.

02 • Latent Space & Score

SVD-PCA & Hybrid Scoring

Aligns 13 shared orbital features with SYBIL’s MATLAB calibration matrix via Z-Score / Domain-Adapted standardization, projects candidates onto SVD-PCA axes (≥ 95% variance) with K-Means (K = 3) geological archetypes, and computes ScoreIRIS blending exponential latent distance (75%) and 4-axis Radar Sub-Scores (25%).

03 • Diagnostic Criterion

Novel Analogue Discovery

Evaluates the diagnostic ratio d / d_best,SYBIL < 1.0 to isolate newly discovered terrestrial terrains closer to a Lunar or Martian target than the best documented analog in SYBIL, exporting Top-25 ranked GeoJSON layers and the interactive viewer below.

Engineering Fidelity & Limitation Warnings

Every analogue match automatically flags unsimulated planetary environmental gaps—including reduced surface gravity (1/6g on the Moon, 0.38g on Mars), ultra-high vacuum, and galactic cosmic radiation—guiding mission planners on when to pair field analog campaigns with cryogenic vacuum chambers or regolith simulants.

How the Coupled SYBIL – IRIS Architecture Works

01 • SYBIL Calibration

Domain-Adapted Harmonization

Using intersect(..., 'stable') and setdiff, MATLAB isolates qualitative metadata and aligns shared numerical parameters (24 features for the Moon, 15 for Mars). For Mars, domain-adapted scaling decouples barometric baselines (~6.36 mbar vs. 1013 mbar) so atmospheric pressure does not mask surface geology.

02 • Bidirectional Matching

Dual-Metric Latent Space

Computes multi-dimensional Euclidean Distances and Cosine Similarities in full PCA space: matching target landing sites to their closest Earth testbed (Planet → Earth) and mapping terrestrial locations back to their extraterrestrial equivalents (Earth → Planet).

03 • IRIS Projection

Global Out-of-Sample Scoring

IRIS standardizes global remote-sensing cells using SYBIL’s exact calibration parameters (μ, σ) and projects them onto SYBIL’s right singular vectors (V), guaranteeing strict mathematical parity between literature analogs and newly discovered sites.

Multi-Test Engineering Site Finder (Part III)

Interactive Mission-Constraint Filtering

Beyond global similarity, SYBIL includes an interactive command-line engine that filters terrestrial sites against strict engineering thresholds—supporting both single-role and combined Multi-Test mission scenarios (with automatic Best-Fit ranking when constraints conflict):

Lunar Mission Filters (6 Modes):

• Touchdown / EDL: Safe area ≥ 200k m², 5m slope ≤ 5°, boulders ≤ 0.35 m, bearing capacity ≥ 5 kPa.
• Rover Mobility: Traverse slope ≤ 8°, cohesion ≥ 0.44 kPa, thermal inertia ≥ 200.
• Permanent Base & Habitats: 25m slope ≤ 4.6°, solar visibility ≥ 70%, DTE ≥ 90%, plume separation ≥ 2 km.
• Lava Tubes (EVA) & ISRU: Pit depth ≥ 20 m, diameter ≥ 35 m, FeO/TiO&sub2; or water-ice content.

Martian Mission Filters (7 Modes):

• EDL & Surface Base: Safe area ≥ 500k m², slope ≤ 5°–10°, thermal inertia ≥ 180, albedo ≤ 0.26.
• UAV / Drone Flight: High-altitude thin-air analogs (≥ 2,500 m elevation, wind ≤ 10 m/s).
• Extreme EVA & ISRU: Hyper-arid cryogenic sites (Tmin ≤ 255 K, RH ≤ 15%) and subsurface permafrost/hydrated sulfates.

Core MATLAB Implementation Snippet

Excerpt from core_matlab/ProvaHackaton7.m — SYBIL Feature Alignment & SVD-PCA Calibration:

% 1. Dynamic quantitative feature intersection (zero hardcoded matrices)
lunar_feats = intersect(T_moon.Properties.VariableNames, T_earth.Properties.VariableNames, 'stable');
lunar_feats = setdiff(lunar_feats, meta_cols, 'stable');

X_Moon  = table2array(T_moon(:, lunar_feats));
X_Earth = table2array(T_earth(:, lunar_feats));

% 2. Z-Score Standardization & SVD Principal Component Analysis
all_data = [X_Earth; X_Moon];
mu = mean(all_data, 1);
sigma = std(all_data, 0, 1); sigma(sigma == 0) = 1e-5;
all_data_norm = (all_data - mu) ./ sigma;

[~, S, V] = svd(all_data_norm - mean(all_data_norm, 1), 'econ');
score_all = all_data_norm * V;
