About the Challenge

Selecting future landing sites and lunar base locations depends on many factors, including topography, geology, environment, and available resources, to name a few. Places on Earth that have analogous characteristics to the Moon or Mars – the Atacama Desert, Haughton Crater, Death Valley – allow mission teams to test equipment and methods before they fly; however, such terrestrial analog sites are not entirely well characterized.

Our challenge is to use open Earth, Moon, and Mars data from NASA and other space agencies to identify and characterize new terrestrial analogs for future bases or landing sites, drawing on deserts, polar regions, volcanic and arid terrains, caves, and other environments that share conditions with the lunar or Martian surface, then present our findings as a map, visualization, or other tool.

How the MATLAB Architecture Works

01 • Data Pipeline

Dynamic Feature Harmonization

Using intersect(..., 'stable') and setdiff, the script automatically isolates qualitative metadata (coordinates, country, dominant mineral) and aligns shared numerical parameters (24 features for the Moon, 15 for Mars), followed by Z-Score standardization.

02 • Matching Engine

Bidirectional Flows (A, B, C, D)

Computes multi-dimensional Euclidean Distances and Cosine Similarities in full PCA space: matching target landing sites to their closest Earth analogue (Flows A & C) and mapping terrestrial sites back to their extraterrestrial equivalents (Flows B & D).

03 • Visual Analytics

3D PCA Cube & Interactive Radar

Generates a synchronized multi-panel dashboard combining an interactive 3D cubic PCA scatter plot, real NASA photogrammetric basemaps, and dynamic Polar Radar Charts (updateRadarPlot) with custom SI-unit DataTips on click.

Multi-Test Engineering Site Finder (Part III)

Interactive Mission-Constraint Filtering

Beyond global similarity, our script includes an interactive command-line engine that filters terrestrial sites against strict engineering thresholds—supporting both single-role and combined Multi-Test mission scenarios (with automatic Best-Fit ranking when constraints conflict):

Lunar Mission Filters (6 Modes):

• Touchdown / EDL: Safe area ≥ 200k m², 5m slope ≤ 5°, boulders ≤ 0.35 m, bearing capacity ≥ 5 kPa.
• Rover Mobility: Traverse slope ≤ 8°, cohesion ≥ 0.44 kPa, thermal inertia ≥ 200.
• Permanent Base & Habitats: 25m slope ≤ 4.6°, solar visibility ≥ 70%, DTE ≥ 90%, plume separation ≥ 2 km.
• Lava Tubes (EVA) & ISRU: Pit depth ≥ 20 m, diameter ≥ 35 m, FeO/TiO&sub2; or water-ice content.

Martian Mission Filters (7 Modes):

• EDL & Surface Base: Safe area ≥ 500k m², slope ≤ 5°–10°, thermal inertia ≥ 180, albedo ≤ 0.26.
• UAV / Drone Flight: High-altitude thin-air analogs (≥ 2,500 m elevation, wind ≤ 10 m/s).
• Extreme EVA & ISRU: Hyper-arid cryogenic sites (Tmin ≤ 255 K, RH ≤ 15%) and subsurface permafrost/hydrated sulfates.

Core MATLAB Implementation Snippet

Excerpt from analog_site_matcher.m — Automated Feature Alignment & SVD-PCA:

% 1. Dynamic quantitative feature intersection (zero hardcoded matrices)
lunar_feats = intersect(T_moon.Properties.VariableNames, T_earth.Properties.VariableNames, 'stable');
lunar_feats = setdiff(lunar_feats, meta_cols, 'stable');

X_Moon  = table2array(T_moon(:, lunar_feats));
X_Earth = table2array(T_earth(:, lunar_feats));

% 2. Z-Score Standardization & SVD Principal Component Analysis
all_data = [X_Earth; X_Moon];
mu = mean(all_data, 1);
sigma = std(all_data, 0, 1); sigma(sigma == 0) = 1e-5;
all_data_norm = (all_data - mu) ./ sigma;

[~, S, V] = svd(all_data_norm - mean(all_data_norm, 1), 'econ');
score_all = all_data_norm * V;
